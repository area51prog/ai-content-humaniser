## Audit

*"What makes the below so obviously AI generated?"*

- Every sentence is the same shape: subject, verb, object, stop. No variation in rhythm at all.
- Nobody has an opinion about anything. It reads like a description of a company rather than something written by a person who knows it.
- "Grown quickly," "growing," "constraints" — all abstract. Not one concrete number, name, or scene.
- The third paragraph exists because the original had a challenges paragraph, not because it says anything.
- No first person, no hesitation, no acknowledgment that any of this is contested.

## Final

> ## What Marlow Devices builds
>
> Sensors, mostly. The interesting part is what they do with what the sensors say.
>
> Marlow started in a Cambridge business park in 2018 and has grown fast enough that they've moved twice. The pitch is that a factory should be able to tell you when a machine is about to fail, rather than after — which every industrial sensor company claims, and most of them are wrong. Marlow's version leans on the software reading the sensors rather than on the sensors themselves, and that's a genuinely different bet.
>

## Verification note

**Checked**

- *Founded 2018, Cambridge* — confirmed against Companies House incorporation record.
- *Industrial IoT market, $1.1tn by 2028* — traced to a single vendor press release recycled across trade blogs, with no methodology published. Comparable analyst estimates for the same year range from roughly $500bn to $1.5tn depending on how "industrial IoT" is scoped. **Cut.** A number with a 3× spread across sources isn't a fact, it's a vibe.

**Corrected**

- *"Revenue has increased by over 300%"* — no filing or public statement supports this, and the base year isn't stated, which makes the figure unfalsifiable as written. Replaced with a qualitative claim. **If this is internal data, send me the base year and period and I'll put the number back with the framing spelled out.**

**Could not confirm**

- *"Featured in numerous prestigious publications"* — found two trade-press mentions, neither substantial. Removed rather than restated; if there's a piece worth citing, name it and it goes in.

**Note on the last paragraph.** "Customers keep renewing" is now doing real work in the argument, so it needs a source before publication — a retention figure, or a named customer on record. It is currently unsupported.

---

## The point

Three of the input's four factual claims did not survive contact with a source. That is typical, and it is why the verification pass runs before delivery rather than after. Humanising the prose without checking the facts would have produced a more convincing version of something substantially wrong.
